Editor workflow in GoodDay

One job, from the moment it lands on you to the moment it is approved. Every step shows you the screen and where to click on it. Read it once, then come back only when something new happens.

This page is only about the work

Logging your day, asking for time off and everything about leave now live in Attendance & Compensation Policy, a separate document for the whole studio. Where files go on Drive is in the Filing system guide. This page is the job, and nothing else.

The coloured steps are the ones where the job is on your desk. On the other steps someone else has it. You do not need to hand it over and you do not need to chase it back. The board does that.

01

A new job

You will be told. You never have to go looking for work on the board.

1

Find your messages

GoodDay does not email you. It tells you things inside the app, in two places, and they show the same things.

A message about a new job looks like this. The blue job name is a link.

If you have never used something like this before

You do not have to check anything on a schedule, and nothing here is a chat you have to keep up with. A job only appears in My Work when it is genuinely your turn, and it leaves by itself when you hand it on. If My Work is empty, there is nothing waiting on you.

2

Read the brief at the top of the job

This is what the client first asked for. It is written once when the job is opened and it never changes, not even after five rounds. That way you can always check what was actually promised.

Notes for one particular round are not here. Those live on that round's ticket, further down under Subtasks. This top box stays as first written.

3

Look at Deadline. That is your date

BoxWhat it meansDoes it move?
DeadlineWhen this round is due back. The only date you work to.Yes, every round
Start / EndWhen you picked it up, and the date the client was promised the finished film.No
EstimateHow big the job was judged to be. Shown in hours. One day is 8 hours.Rarely
The mistake people make

Working to the End date. The End date is weeks away. It is when the client gets the finished film, after every round of notes. Deadline is when this cut is due with your SPV, and it is usually much sooner.

4

If somebody else is cutting it too

Some jobs have two or three editors on them. GoodDay can only track one person per job, so there is a lead editor and there is everybody else, and the job behaves differently depending on which one you are. Look at Assigned to at the top of the job. That name is the lead.

If your name is inThen
Assigned toThe job is yours to drive. You set the status, you send it in at SENT TO SPV, you close the revision tickets. Everything in this guide applies to you.
Other editors on this jobYou are helping on it. The job appears on your board and you get every message about it, so you always know where it is. But you do not change the status and you do not close the tickets. The lead does that.
Do not both move the status

If two people set the status on the same job, the automations fire twice and the round counter goes wrong. One job, one person moving it. If you are not in Assigned to, leave the status alone and tell the lead when your part is done.

If you are helping and the job never appears

Somebody forgot to put you in Other editors on this job. Ask whoever opened the job to add you. It takes them five seconds and the job shows up on your board straight away.

02

While you cut

1

Click the status at the top of the job

2

Choose EDITING

Do this when you start, not when you are halfway. It is how anyone can see what is being worked on right now.

3

If the deadline is tomorrow, you get this message

What to do about it

If you will not make it, tell your SPV the same day you get this message. A day's warning gives them time to move things around. Telling them on the morning it is due does not.

4

Start the timer when you start work

GoodDay can time you while you work, so you never have to remember or guess afterwards. Press start when you sit down, press stop when you get up. That is the whole job.

Step one. Find Time Reports. Open the job. Down the right-hand side is a long column. This is the top of it, and none of this is what you want yet.

↓

keep scrolling to the very bottom of that column

Step two. Press Start timer. The middle of the screen changes. The first time it says No time reports found.

Step three. A small bar sits at the bottom right of your screen while it runs. It shows the job and the clock. You can leave it alone all day.

Step four. Press stop, write one line, submit. A box opens with your time already filled in, so there is no arithmetic.

Start a new timer for each job

The timer belongs to one job. If you switch to a different job, stop the first timer and start one on the second, or the whole afternoon lands on whichever job you happened to open first.

Estimate is a different number, and it is not yours

Further up the same column there is a box called Estimate, usually reading something like 16 hours. That is your SPV's guess, made before you saw the job. It is not a target and you do not correct it. Time Reports is what it really took. The two are meant to be different.

03

Sending it in

One status change. Nothing else.

1

Click the status and choose SENT TO SPV

That is the whole handover. Your SPV gets a message and the job moves into their list. You do not need to tell them in chat or tag anyone.

2

Leave these two boxes alone

Do not fill these in

If you set one of them yourself, the job jumps forward and skips the check it was supposed to get. You never need either box.

After that, stop thinking about it. If it needs more work it comes back to you on its own, with a ticket that says what to change.

04

When it comes back

This part works differently from ClickUp. Read it properly once.

1

You get a message. The job is already back on you

The status will be one of two things:

  • INTERNAL REVISION: your SPV or Julius wants changes. Nobody outside the studio has seen it.
  • CLIENT REVISION: the client has seen it and wants changes.
2

Scroll down to Subtasks and open the newest ticket

3

Read the ticket. It says what to change

4

Go back to the job and check Deadline

The date is not on the ticket

Look at Deadline on the job, not on the revision ticket. One box, one date, every round. That way you never have to work out which of several dates is the live one.

5

Do the work, then set the ticket to ROUND DONE

Open the revision ticket, click its status, and choose ROUND DONE. That status exists only for revision tickets. Never put it on a job.

Log the round's time first

Before you close the ticket, put the hours for this round in Time Reports on the job. Not on the ticket, on the job, so all the rounds add up in one place.

This is the number that shows how much a revision round actually costs. Two rounds that both look like "one round" on the board can be an hour and a day and a half, and only the time says which was which.

6

The job moves itself to SENT TO SPV

If there is still another ticket open on the job, nothing moves yet. Finish that one too.

Every round goes back through SENT TO SPV, including client rounds. Your SPV sees it before the client does, every time.

Two places to read, and neither is yours to edit

The brief at the top of the job is the original ask. It stays as written for the life of the job. Each round's notes are on that round's ticket, in Revision brief, with anything the client sent in Files for this round. Read both. Change neither. If either looks wrong, tell your SPV.

In ClickUp the brief got overwritten every time new client notes arrived, and by round four nobody could see what was first asked for. Now every round keeps its own notes on its own ticket, so round one is still readable at round five.

05

Boxes you never touch

Six of them. Everything else on a job is fine to use.

Things that happen without you

WhenWhat happens by itself
A job is opened with you on itYou get a message with a link to it
You set SENT TO SPVThe job moves to your SPV and they are told
Someone asks for changesThe job comes back to you, a numbered ticket is made and given to you, and you are told
7am the day before a deadlineYou get the due tomorrow message
The job is approvedIt clears out of everyone's list

Action required on a job tells you whose turn it is. If it says you, the job is waiting on you. If it says someone else, it is not.

06

Quick list

  1. Deadline is your date. Not Start/End.
  2. The brief is at the top of the job. Each round's notes are on that round's ticket, in Revision brief.
  3. EDITING when you start. SENT TO SPV when it is ready. Those are your only two status changes.
  4. Revisions arrive as a numbered ticket. Read it, do it, set the ticket to ROUND DONE. The job sends itself back.
  5. Log your hours in Time Reports on the job before you stop for the day. Roughly right is right enough.
  6. Where the files go on Drive is the Filing system guide, not this page.
