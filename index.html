Everything an editor does in GoodDay

Every step here shows you the screen and where to click on it. Read it once. After that, come back only when something new happens.

The coloured steps are the ones where the job is on your desk. On the other steps someone else has it. You do not need to hand it over and you do not need to chase it back. The board does that.

01

Every working day

Monday to Saturday, before you start work. It takes about ten seconds.

1

Click Attendance in the left sidebar

2

Click the Daily Standup tab

3

Fill in the form

Two of these go wrong most often:

  • Your name. Spell it the same way every day. A name typed three different ways looks like three different people to a search box.
  • The job name. Copy it from the Production board. This is what lets anyone pull up every day spent on a job.
4

Set the day to one single day

If you set a range, one entry covers a whole week and the week's numbers come out wrong.

5

Answer how heavy today is, honestly

Say Standby if you are free

Nobody is in trouble for it. Julius reads this to decide who gets the next job. If you say you are busy when you are not, the work goes to someone who is already full.

6

Click Submit

That is the whole morning routine.

02

When you are off

Sick, taking a day, or on leave. Use a different form, and only that one.

1

Click the Time Off Request tab instead

Do not fill in both

If you are not working today, fill in Time Off Request only. Do not also fill in the standup.

2

Say which kind of day it is

  • A day off needs two days' notice. Not tomorrow morning.
  • Sick can be today. File it the same morning. If you have a doctor's note, attach it to the request.
3

Wait. The answer comes to you

The status on your request changes to show the answer:

You asked forStatus becomes
A sick daySICK
A day offDAY OFF
A day off Julius marks as paidPAID LEAVE
Anything he turns downDECLINED
Paid leave is not automatic

Only Julius can mark a day as paid leave, and payroll reads that. If you think a day should be paid leave, write it in the request. Do not assume.

03

A new job

You will be told. You never have to go looking for work on the board.

1

Open the message and click the job name

2

Read the brief at the top of the job

This is what the client first asked for. It is written once when the job is opened and it never changes, not even after five rounds. That way you can always check what was actually promised.

Notes for one particular round are not here. Those go in Revision brief, further down the job, and on that round's ticket. This top box stays as first written.

3

Look at Deadline. That is your date

BoxWhat it meansDoes it move?
DeadlineWhen this round is due back. The only date you work to.Yes, every round
Start / EndWhen you picked it up, and the date the client was promised the finished film.No
EstimateHow big the job was judged to be. Shown in hours. One day is 8 hours.Rarely
The mistake people make

Working to the End date. The End date is weeks away. It is when the client gets the finished film, after every round of notes. Deadline is when this cut is due with your SPV, and it is usually much sooner.

04

While you cut

1

Click the status at the top of the job

2

Choose EDITING

Do this when you start, not when you are halfway. It is how anyone can see what is being worked on right now.

3

If the deadline is tomorrow, you get this message

What to do about it

If you will not make it, tell your SPV the same day you get this message. A day's warning gives them time to move things around. Telling them on the morning it is due does not.

05

Sending it in

One status change. Nothing else.

1

Click the status and choose INTERNAL REVIEW

That is the whole handover. Your SPV gets a message and the job moves into their list. You do not need to tell them in chat or tag anyone.

2

Leave these two boxes alone

Do not fill these in

If you set one of them yourself, the job jumps forward and skips the check it was supposed to get. You never need either box.

After that, stop thinking about it. If it needs more work it comes back to you on its own, with a ticket that says what to change.

06

When it comes back

This part works differently from ClickUp. Read it properly once.

1

You get a message. The job is already back on you

The status will be one of two things:

  • INTERNAL REVISION: your SPV or Julius wants changes. Nobody outside the studio has seen it.
  • CLIENT REVISION: the client has seen it and wants changes.
2

Scroll down to Subtasks and open the newest ticket

3

Read the ticket. It says what to change

4

Go back to the job and check Deadline

The date is not on the ticket

Look at Deadline on the job, not on the revision ticket. One box, one date, every round. That way you never have to work out which of several dates is the live one.

5

Do the work, then set the ticket to ROUND DONE

Open the revision ticket, click its status, and choose ROUND DONE. That status exists only for revision tickets. Never put it on a job.

6

The job moves itself to INTERNAL REVIEW

If there is still another ticket open on the job, nothing moves yet. Finish that one too.

Every round goes back through INTERNAL REVIEW, including client rounds. Your SPV sees it before the client does, every time.

Two briefs, and neither is yours to edit

The brief at the top of the job is the original ask. It stays as written for the life of the job. Revision brief, further down, is what to change this round, and your SPV or Julius rewrites it every time the job comes back. Read both. Change neither. If either looks wrong, tell your SPV.

In ClickUp the brief got overwritten every time new client notes arrived, and by round four nobody could see what was first asked for. That is the habit this split exists to stop.

07

Boxes you never touch

Five of them. Everything else on a job is fine to use.

Things that happen without you

WhenWhat happens by itself
A job is opened with you on itYou get a message with a link to it
You set INTERNAL REVIEWThe job moves to your SPV and they are told
Someone asks for changesThe job comes back to you, a numbered ticket is made and given to you, and you are told
7am the day before a deadlineYou get the due tomorrow message
The job is approvedIt clears out of everyone's list

Action required on a job tells you whose turn it is. If it says you, the job is waiting on you. If it says someone else, it is not.

08

Quick list

  1. Standup every working morning. Same spelling of your name. Honest workload.
  2. Not working? Time Off Request instead. Never both.
  3. Deadline is your date. Not Start/End.
  4. The brief is at the top of the job. Revision brief, further down, is what to change this round.
  5. EDITING when you start. INTERNAL REVIEW when it is ready. Those are your only two status changes.
  6. Revisions arrive as a numbered ticket. Read it, do it, set the ticket to ROUND DONE. The job sends itself back.
